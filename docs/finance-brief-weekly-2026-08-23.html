S&P 500

  • 📉US equities broke three straight weeks of gains: the S&P 500 fell 1.4% on the week to 7,674.37, the Nasdaq lost 2% to 26,180.45 and the Dow slipped 0.9% to 53,277.01 for a second consecutive down week, despite Friday's 0.43% bounce.
    Why The long end of the Treasury market replaced earnings as the dominant equity driver, with the 30-year yield hitting a 19-year high of 5.33% on Tuesday and finishing the week still around 5.25%, repricing long-duration growth stocks — semiconductors first — for five sessions straight.
  • 🏦The week's defining event was a failed rescue of the long bond: Treasury doubled its liquidity-support buybacks to at least $4 billion per operation on Wednesday, knocking 9bps off the 30-year to 5.196%, only for the yield to round-trip to 5.238% within a day and force Bessent back on the tape Friday pledging operations larger still.
    Why Traders read liquidity repurchases of off-the-run paper as a circuit breaker rather than a fix for the supply picture, and Bessent having to escalate within 48 hours confirmed the buyers' strike in long duration is structural — reinforced by July FOMC minutes showing Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all dissented for a hike.
  • 🛒The consumer read deteriorated as the week progressed — Home Depot beat with sales +5.7% to $47.861bn and Target hit a 52-week high on comps of +3.8% and a guidance raise to $9.90-$10.90, but Walmart fell about 9% to roughly $104 on Thursday even after beating on EPS of $0.81 and raising full-year sales guidance.
    Why Walmart's US comparable sales grew just 2.6% versus roughly 3.8% expected and 4.1% the prior quarter — the slowest in about six years — telling investors the guidance raise was funded by tariff refunds and margin timing rather than by household spending.

NIFTY 50

  • 📊India spent the week grinding lower and then going nowhere — the Nifty 50 ended down 0.5% at 24,252.00 and the Sensex 0.6% lower at 77,540.83, with a seven-session losing streak finally snapped on Thursday (+0.64%) before Friday's flat 0.08% close.
    Why The Indian market traded a session behind Wall Street all week, banking Wednesday's US yield relief only on Thursday, by which time the 30-year had already reversed — so the single relief rally had no follow-through.
  • 🛢️Crude was the one driver that never turned: Brent locked in a second straight weekly gain of around 6% to roughly $93.50, having traded as high as $95.40, as transits through the Strait of Hormuz stayed in single digits for the entire week after eight vessel attacks this month.
    Why Trump confirmed the naval blockade remains in effect with no live negotiations, then escalated to an 'economic D-Day' sanctions campaign against Tehran due Monday, so the shipping risk premium never got a diplomatic off-ramp to price out.
  • 💱Domestic money again did all the work while the currency stood still — DIIs bought ₹5,101.50cr, ₹3,973.70cr, ₹3,537.70cr and ₹2,124.10cr across the week against net FII selling every session bar Wednesday's ₹408cr, and the rupee held a 30-paisa range around 95.70, settling near 95.7050.
    Why Steady SIP-funded domestic buying absorbed foreign outflows driven by the US rate differential, capping the drawdown at 0.5%, while rising oil and importer hedging pulled the rupee down exactly as much as RBI intervention and a softer dollar pushed it up.

Crypto

  • ₿Crypto decoupled violently from the rates backdrop — Bitcoin rose roughly 22-24% on the week, running from about $62,700 on Monday to a high of $79,400 on Friday before stalling below $80,000 and settling near $77,000-77,700, its best five-day run since March 2023 and a clean break of the $62,000-$66,900 range that had held since July 8.
    Why The Treasury's buyback announcement removed the rates pressure capping risk assets at exactly the moment Washington delivered back-to-back crypto policy wins, turning a market positioned bearishly for four days of ETF outflows into a chase.
  • 💥The move was mechanically driven rather than organic — cumulative short liquidations passed $4 billion over the surge, including $1.14bn in a single hour on Wednesday (the largest day of short destruction ever recorded) and another $1bn on Friday, adding roughly $280bn to total crypto market cap, which closed the week 6.8% higher at $2.6 trillion.
    Why Bearish leverage built during the earlier four-day, $332 million ETF outflow stretch was crowded enough that each liquidation cluster triggered the next, and bears who re-shorted the $71,000 breakout were stopped out again on the run to $79,400.
  • ⚖️Three regulatory catalysts landed in five days — Treasury's GENIUS Act stablecoin rulemaking (comment period to October 19), the SEC's first substantive crypto rulemaking in 'Regulation Crypto Assets' with its conditional securities safe harbour, and Trump's White House push for the Clarity Act ahead of a September 15 Senate procedural vote — and the ETF bid followed, with spot bitcoin funds taking $606 million on Thursday and $827 million flowing across all listed crypto products in one session.
    Why Allocators read the combination as a regime shift rather than a trade and began buying the whole asset class, which is why token-issuing networks with unresolved securities status repriced hardest — XRP +19.6% to $1.42, Ether up about 25% on the week to $2,402.78 and Solana to $91.64.
