S&P 500

  • 📈The index round-tripped a two-day slide into a record and back, opening the week at 7,753.11, bottoming at 7,728.20 on Tuesday, printing a record close of 7,798.99 on Thursday — its 27th of the year — and settling Friday at 7,785.76 for a third straight weekly gain, with the Russell 2000 also closing at a fresh record.
    Why Two soft inflation prints in two days — July CPI exactly in line at 3.4% headline and 2.5% core, then a flat July PPI with the annual rate cooling to 4.7% from 5.5% — undid the early-week oil-driven inflation scare.

NIFTY 50

  • 📉India went the other way from Wall Street all week: the Nifty 50 fell 204.65 points (-0.83% week-on-week) to 24,366.00 and the Sensex lost 489.92 points (-0.62%) to 78,009.25, snapping a two-week winning streak in the same week the S&P 500 logged its third straight advance.
    Why Brent's 5%-plus weekly gain on the failure of the Iran-Oman Hormuz talks and Washington's threat to sustain its naval blockade indefinitely revived the import-bill and rupee arithmetic that capped every Indian rally this month.
  • 🌡️Both halves of India's inflation picture worsened underneath the headlines: July retail inflation rose to 4.45% from 4.38% — a second straight month above the RBI's 4% target, with food inflation up to 5.52% — while July WPI cooled only marginally to 9.78% from 9.87%, as Primary Articles jumped to 8.52% from 7.0% and Manufactured Products to 8.29% from 7.48%.
    Why The wholesale headline fell purely because the fuel base effect faded — Fuel and Power decelerated to 20.05% from 27.41% — masking the crude shock passing through into food and manufactured goods, a far stickier problem for the RBI than an energy spike.
  • 🏛️Two corporate governance shocks compounded the macro drag: N Chandrasekaran will not seek reappointment as Tata Sons chairman when his term ends in February 2027, triggering a Tata-group selloff that carried Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles down 4.46% to ₹334 by Friday, while Godrej Consumer Products crashed 10% to a 52-week low of ₹916.20 on the abrupt exit of MD and CEO Sudhir Sitapati. Domestic institutions kept absorbing the selling, buying ₹5,841.66 crore on Wednesday and ₹4,353.10 crore on Thursday against modest FPI sales.
    Why Both departures were unexplained to the market, so investors priced strategy discontinuity rather than routine succession, and Thursday's 1.82% Tata Motors bounce proved a one-session dead-cat rally.
